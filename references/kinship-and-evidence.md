# Establish a relationship

Start with a proposition narrow enough to test, such as “Was the bride in the 1872 parish register the daughter of the miller in the 1860 census?” Write down another explanation that would fit the starting evidence. Two people with similar names can live in the same small town.

## Trace one generation

- Anchor the younger person's identity through records that overlap in time. Compare spouses, children, occupation, addresses, witnesses, neighbors and signatures. Distinguish the date of an event from the date it was reported. Preserve name variants alongside the person's display name.
- Target records that could name both people and their relationship. Try a birth or baptism entry, marriage application, probate distribution, guardianship, pension dependency affidavit, family register, or a deed identifying heirs. Read the surrounding pages and any continuation. A census household can include boarders, stepchildren, employees and several families. Check whom its relationship column refers to.
- Ask who supplied each detail, when, and how they could know it. A death certificate can give a physician's firsthand account of death and a son-in-law's recollection of the parents. Trace a tree, index or repeated obituary back to its source. Ten copies of one statement preserve one account.
- If no document states the relationship, show the chain of indirect evidence, explain how the records identify the same people, and address credible alternatives. Record disagreements without silently choosing a date, name or parent. Use plain statuses such as **documented**, **probable**, **possible** and **unresolved**, with a reason. Assign status to each relationship or claim.
- For descent before 1700, look up the parish, court, language, denominations, calendars and surviving records of the place at the time. Use local equivalents of parish registers, notarial acts, manorial or land records, tax rolls and probate. Track witnesses, godparents, neighbors and land across generations. Separate a gap in surviving records from a search that has yet to be done.
- For a common ancestor, place both chains side by side and cite each link. Identify the first unsupported step on either side. If one person appears in both branches, compare the underlying identities before joining the trees. Compute a cousin relationship only for a supported path.

When an early branch seems to jump a generation, work toward the gap from both ends. Start below it with the last securely identified child. Start above it with a dated record of the proposed ancestor. Ask what transaction might name both households. A will may name a married daughter, a partition may identify her children, and a later deed may identify the share they sold. Follow the parties and the description of the parcel through those transactions. A land grant can firmly place the proposed ancestor at an early date while leaving the descent entirely open. Keep that separately documented household visible as a candidate.

## Keep a compact claim log

| Field | What to enter |
| --- | --- |
| Claim | Stable person IDs, precise assertion and relationship type or direction |
| Evidence | Source ID, original wording or faithful abstract, page/image/entry, informant |
| Source | Creator, title or collection, repository, date, stable public URL, access date, local copy or retrieval status |
| Assessment | Status, reasoning, conflicting evidence and viable alternatives |
| Follow-up | Specific record, jurisdiction and date range that could settle the question |

For a negative search, log the repository, collection, actual coverage, names and variants tried, date searched, and whether images or an index were checked. Give the absence weight only if the event would ordinarily appear there and relevant records survive.

For an unindexed volume, use the archive's catalogue to select the court or parish, series and year span. Check the front and back for a handwritten index. Follow the entry's original book and folio numbers into the images, then inspect overflow pages and later settlements. Save both folio and image number so the next reader can retrace the search.
