# From a remembered trade to a family story

**Fictional teaching example.** All people, documents, quotations and places below are invented. The source IDs illustrate how a reader can follow a conclusion back to a page.

A researcher remembers, “Gran Ora looked after the lamps. I think Elias was her father.” The questions are whether Ora Vale Bell was a daughter of Elias Vale, and what she actually did at the signal station.

| ID | Record examined | Relevant detail |
| --- | --- | --- |
| S1 | North Weir station housing return, 1908, dwelling 12, lines 4–6 | Elias Vale, station keeper, lists Ora Vale, 17, as his niece. |
| S2 | Ridge District marriage register, 1912, book B, folio 39, entry 8 | Ora Vale of the station cottage, daughter of Ruth Vale, marries Ivo Bell. The father's field is blank. |
| S3 | Estate of Elias Vale, 1916, signed will, folio 12 verso, clause 4 | He leaves his signal lamp to “my niece Ora Bell, child of my sister Ruth.” |
| S4 | North Weir station log, 1914, May 9, folio 21 | “Mrs O. Bell relit the east lamp at dusk.” The keeper signs the entry. |
| S5 | Harbor Gazette, May 14, 1914, p. 2, col. 3 | A correspondent names Ora Bell of the station cottage as the woman who restored the east lamp during that week's fog. A second paper reprints this item verbatim. |

**Decide the kinship claim.** The cottage and marriage entry connect Ora Vale to Ora Bell. S2 names Ruth as her mother. S3 independently calls Ruth Elias’s sister and Ora his niece, in agreement with S1. Add the mother–daughter edge with S2 and S3 and the sibling edge between Ruth and Elias with S3. Preserve the recollection in the casebook. Leave Ora’s father unresolved. Seek the district birth register around 1890–92. The blank marriage field alone gives no reason for the omission.

**Decide the occupational claim.** S4 gives an initial, one action and a date. S5 supplies her given name, residence and the same unusual action. Together they support her identification and that evening's event. Only Elias has a verified job title. A payroll, appointment or a run of signed duty logs could establish whether Ora regularly held a paid post. The copied newspaper item adds circulation history to S5.

**Write for the family.** On May 9, 1914, Ora Bell relit the east signal lamp at dusk. The keeper recorded the act in his log. Five days later a local correspondent named the woman from the station cottage who had kept the lamp burning in the fog. Two years later her uncle Elias left her his signal lamp. [S4, fol. 21; S5, p. 2, col. 3; S3, fol. 12v, cl. 4.]

In a live project, link those locators to the saved pages and repository entries. If the newspaper image is temporarily unavailable, preserve exactly which representation was read and put the page in the shared queue. When recovered, inspect the column and replace the interim citation.
