# Present a tree and the lives in it

Match the output to the request. A short, sourced answer may settle a kinship question. A longer project may warrant an explorer and timeline that another relative can use without learning the underlying data format.

## Tree explorer

- Give each person a stable identity. Keep partnerships, parent–child relationships, siblings and other associations as separately typed connections, each linked to its evidence and confidence. Label biological, adoptive and step relationships when known. Preserve the source's wording when the kind of relationship remains open.
- For a generational graph, place partners horizontally and parents above children. Use labeled dotted lines for sibling or other documented same-generation ties. Sharing a generation alone does not establish a relationship. Give the lines a legend and accessible text labels. Preserve half-sibling and disputed links, repeated ancestors, remarriages, people with no known parents and separate family groups.
- Open a detail panel or modal for each person. Include a brief life narrative, relationships with citations, dated events, portraits or clippings and links to the retained artifact and repository entry. Let a reader tell which page identifies a parent and which supplies a story about that parent.

## Timeline and map

Give each event a person, date or interval, place and precision, description, and source locator. Preserve events with unknown locations in the timeline. Keep county-level places at county precision. For a map with playback, highlight the event and corresponding location together, allow pause and direct selection, and disclose how overlapping or loosely dated events are ordered. Use the evidence for travel between places. Two newspaper datelines alone establish no journey.

## Write a story someone will want to open

Choose a scene the record lets the reader picture. A signature at the bottom of a petition, a note pencilled on a concert bill or the wording of a small advertisement can carry more of the person's voice than a list of memberships. Identify the actor, place and occasion, then give a short attributed quotation or observed detail. Explain whether a witness recalled the scene years later, a reporter described it the next day or an organizer only announced it in advance. Pair the story with a legible excerpt. Link through to the full page so readers can inspect the caption, column or surrounding signatures themselves.

## Check what the reader sees

Compare the roster to the rendered people, including disconnected groups. Trace one documented link and one uncertain link to their citations. Check the directed parent–child subgraph for cycles. Partnership and sibling edges are lateral. When siblings are inferred from a shared parent, carry the evidence for both parent links, and identify half-siblings only when the other parentage is established. Open a retained image and verify its caption, page and source. Pick an approximate date and a locationless event and follow each through the display. Check distribution rights and living-person visibility for the intended audience. Keep the archival evidence separate from derivatives prepared for sharing.


For an offline explorer, keep the evidence folder beside the viewer and use relative links. Try one portrait, its full page, a relationship citation, a map event and play/pause from a freshly opened copy. Test a disconnected person and someone who appears in two pedigree paths. When sharing outside the family, generate the release from the shareable subset. Examine the embedded data, downloadable files, image metadata and captions as well as what the interface displays.
