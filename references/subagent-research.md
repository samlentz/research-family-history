# Working with research subagents

Use parallel research when each person can answer a different question from a common starting point. For example, one researcher can identify a parent in probate records while another reconstructs the child's working life from trade directories and the local press. The lead researcher keeps the master tree, the family-facing account and the shared retrieval queue.

## Choose the split

| Situation | Assignment |
| --- | --- |
| A proposed common ancestor | Give each researcher one firmly identified descendant and ask them to work backward. Have each cite every link and name the first gap. Compare the two paths before joining them. |
| A person with a promising biographical clue | Give one researcher the identity question and another the activity that generated records. Define the jurisdiction, years and collections. A new employer, alias or address found by either researcher goes to the other promptly. |
| A difficult reading or disputed identification | Ask for independent readings of the same page. Supply the image, its context and a neutral question. Compare the passages and reasons before revealing the other reading. Two readings of one record still represent one source. |
| The only lead is a common name | Establish an anchor first, such as a household, trade or spouse. Splitting a weak match across several generations can multiply the same error. |

Start with the smallest team that covers the independent questions. As evidence arrives, narrow or retire overlapping assignments. The lead can pursue a separate collection while others work.

## A brief to copy

> Investigate **[one question]** about **[person ID, name variants, approximate dates and place]**. Start from **[source ID and page]**. The open identity question is **[ambiguity]**. Cover **[collections, jurisdiction and years]**. Another researcher owns **[adjacent work]**. Follow the project's **[web, contact, subscription, privacy and download limits]**. Save permitted evidence and your findings in **[assigned folder]**. Flag a new identifier, conflicting record or access limit as soon as it affects another assignment. Return when you have evidence that answers the question or can name the specific obstacle and strongest next lead.

Share only the family details needed for that assignment. When agents share a workspace, give each one its own notes and evidence files. Let the lead integrate shared records. If agents use the same browser tab or draw on the same limited download allowance, assign one person to operate it.

In the [fictional worked case](worked-case.md), one researcher could test how Ora Vale was related to Elias, using the marriage register and estate file. Another could investigate who tended the signal lamp, using station logs and press accounts. The first should send the married surname **Bell** as soon as it appears, with the marriage entry. That lets the second researcher search for Ora in a different set of records while the estate work continues.

## What to return

- **Finding and confidence.** State the precise relationship, event or quotation, who supplied it and what identifies the person. Separate the source's words from the inference. Include a plausible competing identity when it affects the result.
- **Evidence another person can open.** Give the creator, title or collection, date, page or image and entry or column, stable locator, and local path where permitted. Say whether you inspected an original image, transcription, index or snippet. Identify the parent page of a crop.
- **Unfinished work.** Record collection coverage, variants and pages checked for a meaningful negative search. For a blocked item, report the locator, last attempt, access condition, next eligible retry and what the item could settle. A handoff in the conversation is enough for a short task. Save it in the assigned folder when others will resume it.

## Reconcile before updating the tree

1. Compare the actual cited passages. Check the underlying page for a new parent, an ambiguous identity, a quotation or a portrait caption when available. Retain any access qualification. Match the person using identifiers that distinguish local namesakes.
2. Reconcile duplicate finds by the underlying record. Two scans of one register entry get one evidence citation with alternate access links. A newspaper reprint may deserve its own publication citation, but it inherits the original account. Independent sources can disagree. Preserve both readings and explain the consequence for the claim.
3. Merge people, typed relationships, events and source IDs once. Preserve competing candidates and leave unresolved links open. If two accounts agree a person worked at a mill but disagree on the year they became foreman, the biography can report the mill work while the promotion date stays open.
4. Consolidate blocked items by repository and item or page in the **one shared queue**. Give each item one retry owner. Honor a provider's cooldown across agents sharing that access. Continue with other collections meanwhile. After an eligible retry, verify the recovered content and update the citation and queue together. If the block persists, leave a dated next step. Schedule a future retry only when requested and available.

Deliver one synthesized account with direct citations, the strongest remaining uncertainty and the next record worth opening. Keep the branch handoffs available for anyone who wants to retrace the work.
