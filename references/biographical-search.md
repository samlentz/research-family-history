# Find the records a life produced

Turn each remembered detail into a search for a particular record. A remembered blue ribbon suggests an exhibition, a sponsoring society and a published list of prizewinners. Search the remembered phrase with the town and decade first. Learn the event's formal name and sponsor, then search the results pages for the person. A scheduled entry, a qualifying result and a final award support different claims.

| Starting clue | Where to look and what to verify |
| --- | --- |
| Education or a class photograph | Try university archives, student newspapers, annual catalogues, yearbooks, commencement programs and alumni notes. Check campus, term, school and name variants, including the spouse's surname and initials. Cite exactly what the record shows about attendance, course of study, graduation or a degree. Keep the caption with the photograph and verify its row, order and orientation. |
| Trade, profession, invention | Search city directories across multiple years, trade journals, association proceedings, technical papers, employers' catalogues and patent-office records. Combine the technology with the location and surname. Check inventor, assignee, employer, dates and matching biographical details. An installation report or a talk may document skilled work even when a patent search yields nothing. |
| Military service | Check enlistments, pension and dependency files, muster rolls, contemporary letters and unit histories. Separate the veteran's own role, duty station and dates from the unit's movements. Qualify recollections, service claims and awards according to who recorded them. |
| Community life and quotations | Try local columns, club minutes, church publications, interviews, reunion reports and signed reminiscences. Search addresses, schools, businesses, clubs and companions alongside name variants. Nearby towns may have carried a better account. Attribute memories to the speaker and give the publication date and the period remembered. |
| Photographs or illustrations | Search named collections, class cohorts, professional associations and caption text. Prefer the best available original with identification and credit. A group picture can establish appearance or participation when its caption identifies the person. Appearance alone is insufficient identification. |

## Search beyond the first hit

Use the first clipping to learn what the school, society or employer called itself at the time. Then switch repositories. A home-town departure notice can identify the campus. Its directory can identify a dorm, class year and course abbreviation. Check the directory's key. A medal citation, an athletics report or a society column can then yield the scene that makes the biography worth reading. Catalogue descriptions of a degree program describe what students were offered. A transcript, degree roll or registrar's list can establish what this student completed.

When a volume is downloadable, search the whole thing for the subject, their siblings and a future spouse, as well as nicknames or a distinctive hometown. The optional [volume search tool](../scripts/search_volume.py) prints one-based PDF page numbers and nearby indexed text. For example, run `python3 scripts/search_volume.py annual.pdf "Ora Vale" "Ora Vail" "North Weir"` from the skill folder. It uses an available `pdftotext` or Python's `pypdf`; a UTF-8 text file with form-feed page breaks needs neither. Read the matching **page image**, identify the section and count rows or columns in the order its caption specifies. Keep both printed and viewer pagination. For image-only pages, browse the alphabetical section, contemporary index or available OCR. Record that gap if reporting a negative search.

## Read the whole page

Search initials, nicknames, former surnames, spouses' initials and spelling variants. Break phrases into rare words or use a contemporary street, institution or teammate. When OCR misses, browse issues around the event and the same weekly column. Check page headers, columns, continuations, captions and whether a second newspaper reprinted the first. Save the full page with publication, edition, date, page and column. Derive a labeled crop for display, retaining the original. Quote a distinctive short passage in context. Record the event date separately if the article supplies it.

To find the story someone else told about the family, search the names of neighbors, former pupils, shopkeepers and fellow veterans. Try the hometown alongside phrases such as “I remember,” “old residents” or “recollections.” Once a columnist turns up, browse earlier and later installments under the byline. A series can explain a nickname or describe a day's work even if the family's surname occurs only once. When writing the result, name the observer, say how they knew the family if the source tells you, and preserve one concrete scene in their words.

## Recover an artifact

Maintain **one shared retrieval queue**. Record the source ID, item URL or archive call number, page/issue, evidence sought, access condition, last attempt, next eligible retry and any alternate repository. For a rate limit, honor the site's wait interval, then try again later. Search another repository or continue another person meanwhile. When the copy is saved and checked, replace the pending status with its path. If work is delegated, every researcher uses this queue.

Use authorized access for a subscription. Follow the researcher's instructions and the site's permitted access flow for sign-in or CAPTCHA. A search snippet or catalogue entry remains a lead until its contents can be inspected. If saving or sharing a copy is restricted, retain the citation, page locator, access status and permitted notes. For living people, preserve the minimum personal information needed for the agreed deliverable.

Distinguish a temporary quota from an access restriction, a missing scan and an item nobody has digitized. After a temporary limit, wait for the published interval or a reasonable cooldown and make a bounded retry. Check an authorized viewer or another repository's copy of the same issue. Open the saved file and verify the page, date and content before closing its queue entry. Replace the interim reading-note link wherever readers encounter it. If the restriction persists, leave the last result and a specific next action in the queue. Schedule a future attempt only with authorization and an available scheduler.
